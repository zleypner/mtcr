## What Is Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty is a surgical procedure to reshape the nose for cosmetic or functional purposes. It's one of the most common and technically demanding facial plastic surgeries, requiring both surgical skill and artistic vision.

### Cosmetic Goals

- Reduce or increase nose size
- Change nose shape (bridge, tip, nostrils)
- Correct asymmetry
- Refine nasal tip
- Improve overall facial harmony

### Functional Goals

- Correct breathing difficulties
- Repair deviated septum
- Address structural issues
- Improve airway function

## Types of Rhinoplasty

### Primary Rhinoplasty

First-time nose surgery:

- Most common type
- Addresses cosmetic and/or functional concerns
- Predictable outcomes
- Standard recovery

### Revision Rhinoplasty

Correction of previous surgery:

- More complex procedure
- May require cartilage grafts
- Longer recovery possible
- Higher cost
- Requires experienced revision specialist

### Septorhinoplasty

Combines cosmetic reshaping with septum correction:

- Addresses both form and function
- Single surgery for dual purposes
- May be partially covered by insurance
- Popular choice for comprehensive correction

### Ethnic Rhinoplasty

Specialized techniques for diverse facial features:

- Preserves ethnic identity
- Enhances while maintaining natural look
- Requires cultural sensitivity
- Specialized expertise needed

### Non-Surgical Rhinoplasty

Dermal filler alternative (temporary):

- No surgery required
- Quick procedure (15-30 minutes)
- Temporary results (6-18 months)
- Limited corrections possible
- Good for minor refinements

## The Procedure

### Pre-Operative Planning

**Virtual Consultation:**
- Share photos (front, side, three-quarter views)
- Discuss goals and expectations
- Review medical history
- Receive preliminary recommendations
- Computer imaging of potential results (some surgeons)

**In-Person Consultation:**
- Physical examination
- Detailed discussion of technique
- Review of imaging simulations
- Final surgical plan
- Pre-operative instructions

### Day of Surgery

**Before Surgery:**
- Arrive fasting (typically 8+ hours)
- Pre-operative blood work completed
- Meet with anesthesiologist
- Final markings and review
- IV placement and monitoring

**During Surgery (2-4 hours):**

1. General anesthesia administered
2. Incisions made (open or closed approach)
3. Bone and cartilage reshaped
4. Septum corrected if needed
5. Incisions closed
6. Splint applied

**Open vs. Closed Approach:**

- **Closed:** All incisions inside nose, no visible scarring, limited visibility for surgeon
- **Open:** Small incision on columella (between nostrils), better visibility, minimal scarring

### Immediate Post-Operative

- Recovery room monitoring (1-2 hours)
- Possible overnight stay
- Pain managed with medication
- Head elevation important
- Splint and packing in place

## Recovery Timeline

### Week 1: Initial Recovery

- **Days 1-3:** Most swelling and bruising
- Keep head elevated at all times
- Cold compresses around eyes (not on nose)
- Soft diet, avoid chewing hard foods
- Take prescribed medications
- No blowing nose

**What to Expect:**
- Significant swelling and bruising
- Nasal congestion
- Mild to moderate discomfort
- Sleep disturbances (mouth breathing)

### Week 2: Splint Removal

- **Day 7-10:** Splint and stitches removed
- First glimpse of new nose shape
- Still significant swelling
- Most bruising resolving
- May return to light activities
- Most patients fly home after day 10-14

### Weeks 3-4: Continued Healing

- Swelling continues to decrease
- Return to most normal activities
- Avoid strenuous exercise
- No contact sports
- Glasses cannot rest on nose (use tape or straps)
- Sun protection important

### Months 2-6: Refinement

- Noticeable improvement in swelling
- Nose shape becoming clearer
- Minor swelling may persist
- Final shape emerging

### Months 6-12: Final Results

- Most swelling resolved
- Final shape visible
- Skin fully adhered
- Enjoy your new profile

**Note:** Tip swelling may persist longest, especially with thick skin. Final results can take up to 18 months.

## Am I a Candidate?

### Ideal Candidates

- Physically healthy
- Non-smoker (or willing to quit)
- Realistic expectations
- Facial growth complete (typically 15-16 for women, 17-18 for men)
- Specific, well-defined goals
- Good skin elasticity

### Considerations

- **Thick skin:** May limit tip definition
- **Previous surgery:** May require revision techniques
- **Unrealistic expectations:** Discuss thoroughly with surgeon
- **Certain medical conditions:** May increase risks

### Not Ideal Candidates

- Active smokers unwilling to quit
- Unrealistic expectations
- Unstable psychological state
- Certain medical conditions
- Very young patients (facial growth incomplete)

## Risks and Complications

### Common (Expected)

- Swelling and bruising
- Nasal congestion
- Minor asymmetry (often resolves)
- Temporary numbness

### Less Common

- Infection
- Bleeding
- Scarring issues
- Difficulty breathing
- Need for revision surgery
- Unsatisfactory results

### Minimizing Risks

- Choose experienced, board-certified surgeon
- Follow all pre and post-operative instructions
- Stop smoking well before surgery
- Disclose complete medical history
- Attend all follow-up appointments

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will rhinoplasty be painful?

Most patients describe discomfort rather than pain. The nose itself has limited sensation after surgery. Congestion and pressure are more common complaints. Pain medication manages any discomfort effectively.

### How long until I see final results?

While you'll see improvement once the splint is removed, final results take 6-12 months as swelling gradually resolves. The tip takes longest to refine.

### Will there be visible scars?

With closed rhinoplasty, all incisions are inside the nose. Open rhinoplasty leaves a small scar on the columella that typically fades to near-invisibility.

### Can rhinoplasty fix my breathing?

Yes. Septorhinoplasty addresses both cosmetic concerns and breathing issues. Discuss functional goals during your consultation.

### What if I don't like the results?

Choose your surgeon carefully and communicate clearly about goals. If revision is needed, most surgeons have policies for addressing concerns. Revision surgery is possible but more complex.

### When can I exercise again?

Light walking after 2 weeks, moderate exercise at 4-6 weeks, contact sports at 3 months minimum. Follow your surgeon's specific guidance.
