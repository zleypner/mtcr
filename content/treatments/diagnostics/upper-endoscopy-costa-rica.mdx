## What Is an Upper Endoscopy?

An upper endoscopy, also called an esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) or gastroscopy, is an exam that allows doctors to see inside your stomach using a thin, flexible tube with a camera at the tip. The endoscope is gently inserted through the mouth, allowing the doctor to visualize:

- The **esophagus** (the tube connecting your mouth to your stomach)
- The **stomach**
- The **duodenum** (the first part of your small intestine)

It's the most reliable way to know what's happening when you have persistent stomach problems.

## What Is Upper Endoscopy Used For?

Doctors use upper endoscopy to:

- **Detect gastritis or ulcers**: Visualize inflammation or sores in the stomach lining
- **Look for H. pylori bacteria**: A common cause of stomach problems that can be identified through biopsies
- **Find the cause of persistent heartburn or reflux**: See if acid damage has occurred in the esophagus
- **Investigate unexplained stomach pain**: Identify the source of chronic discomfort
- **Locate bleeding sources**: Find where digestive bleeding originates
- **Detect esophageal problems**: Including narrowing, Barrett's esophagus, or other conditions

The procedure also allows the gastroenterologist to take small tissue samples (biopsies) for laboratory analysis when needed.

## How Do I Prepare for an Upper Endoscopy?

Preparation is straightforward:

- **Fasting**: Don't eat anything for several hours before the exam (your doctor will provide exact timing, typically 6-8 hours)
- **Medications**: Inform your doctor about all medications you take, especially blood thinners
- **Transportation**: Arrange for someone to drive you home afterward, as sedation will affect your ability to drive
- **Comfortable clothing**: Wear loose, comfortable clothes to your appointment

Your medical team will provide complete preparation instructions specific to your situation.

## What Is the Procedure Like?

The upper endoscopy follows a simple, well-established process:

1. **Sedation**: You receive medication to help you relax and prevent discomfort. Most patients feel drowsy and comfortable.

2. **The examination**: The doctor gently inserts the thin, flexible endoscope through your mouth. You won't feel it thanks to the sedation.

3. **Quick procedure**: The actual examination typically takes only 15-30 minutes.

4. **Recovery**: You rest briefly in a recovery area, then can go home the same day.

## Does Upper Endoscopy Hurt?

**No.** With the sedation medication provided, most people don't feel anything during the procedure and often don't remember it afterward.

The only thing you might notice after waking up is a slightly irritated throat, which typically resolves within a few hours. Some patients experience mild bloating from the air used during the procedure, but this passes quickly.

## Is Upper Endoscopy Safe?

Upper endoscopy is a very safe procedure. Serious complications are extremely rare—occurring in less than 1 in every 2,500 procedures.

The procedure is performed by trained gastroenterologists using modern equipment with built-in safety features. The benefits of accurate diagnosis far outweigh the minimal risks involved.

## What Happens After the Procedure?

Post-procedure care is simple:

- **Rest**: Take it easy for the rest of the day
- **No driving**: The sedation medication can make you drowsy for several hours, so don't drive or operate machinery
- **Initial results**: Your doctor will typically share what they observed when you wake up from sedation
- **Biopsy results**: If tissue samples were taken, laboratory results are usually available within a few days
- **Resume eating**: You can typically eat normally once the sedation wears off and you feel comfortable swallowing

## When Should I Call the Doctor After?

Contact your healthcare provider if you experience:

- Severe or worsening stomach pain
- Fever
- Difficulty swallowing that gets worse rather than better
- Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds
- Black, tarry stools

These symptoms are rare but should be evaluated promptly.

## Common Conditions Diagnosed with Upper Endoscopy

### Gastritis
Inflammation of the stomach lining that can cause pain, nausea, and discomfort. Endoscopy allows direct visualization and biopsy to determine the cause.

### Peptic Ulcers
Sores in the stomach or duodenal lining. Endoscopy can identify ulcers, assess severity, and sometimes treat bleeding ulcers.

### Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)
Chronic acid reflux that may damage the esophagus. Endoscopy reveals the extent of damage and helps guide treatment.

### H. Pylori Infection
A bacterial infection that causes many stomach problems. Biopsies taken during endoscopy can confirm the diagnosis.

### Barrett's Esophagus
A condition where esophageal cells change due to chronic acid exposure. Regular endoscopic monitoring is important for affected patients.

### Celiac Disease
An immune reaction to gluten that damages the small intestine. Duodenal biopsies during endoscopy help confirm diagnosis.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does an upper endoscopy take?

The procedure itself typically takes 15-30 minutes. Plan for about 2 hours total, including preparation and recovery time.

### Will I be completely unconscious?

Most upper endoscopies use conscious sedation, meaning you're deeply relaxed but not fully unconscious. You likely won't remember the procedure.

### Can I eat before my endoscopy?

No. You'll need to fast for 6-8 hours before the procedure to ensure your stomach is empty for clear visualization.

### How soon can I return to normal activities?

Most patients can resume normal activities the day after the procedure. However, you should not drive, work, or make important decisions the day of the procedure due to sedation effects.

### Is upper endoscopy the same as a colonoscopy?

No. Upper endoscopy examines the upper digestive tract (esophagus, stomach, duodenum), while [colonoscopy](/treatments/diagnostics/colonoscopy-costa-rica) examines the lower digestive tract (colon and rectum). Different instruments are used, entering through different routes.

### How often do I need an upper endoscopy?

This depends on your condition and findings. Some patients need only one diagnostic endoscopy, while others with conditions like Barrett's esophagus may need periodic surveillance endoscopies.
